Subject: SMC20D17H5 documentation for low-cost underwater chirp ranging prototype

Hello,

We are evaluating SMC20D17H5 for a student-built underwater acoustic communication
and positioning prototype. Our target is approximately 20 m communication range
with a 1 m receiver baseline. The receivers will use a Sony Spresense at 192 ksample/s.
Operating depth has not yet been finalized; the 20 m figure is communication range.

Before selecting the transmit driver and encapsulation process, could you please
provide the current datasheet/drawing and clarify the following?

1. Maximum continuous and burst drive voltage (Vrms/Vpp), allowable duty cycle,
   current, pulse duration, and any frequency or water-loading restrictions.
2. Complex electrical impedance versus frequency in water after a recommended
   encapsulation process, including the minimum impedance. We understand that
   the published resonance impedance of <=12 ohms is not a lower bound.
3. Transmit voltage response and receiving voltage sensitivity versus frequency,
   useful chirp bandwidth, directivity, and unit-to-unit tolerances, if available.
4. Recommended coating/potting material, thickness, curing conditions, electrode
   soldering temperature/time, cable attachment and strain relief. What immersion
   depth/pressure and duration have been tested for such an assembly?
5. The present product page specifies 57 +/-2 kHz. A 2019 publication using this
   part reports operation near 47 kHz. Has the part changed, or are these likely
   different loading/encapsulation conditions? We do not assume these are equivalent.
6. Current two-piece pack price, lead time, and shipping availability to Japan.
   Is there a comparably inexpensive part with documented broader underwater
   bandwidth that you would recommend for this application?

We plan to evaluate a small pair first and only expand to a multi-receiver system
after measuring the transfer response. We are not requesting a guarantee of our
system-level positioning accuracy, only the component data needed for a sound design.

Thank you.
